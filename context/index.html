AI assistant.

earmarkIQ Context is a live, read-only MCP server. Connect your earmarkIQ data to Claude — verified — or any MCP-capable assistant, and ask about your own finances with answers grounded in your real numbers rather than general knowledge.

It can read. It cannot act. Not because we have promised not to, but because no capability to act exists inside it — and a test in our build fails if anyone ever adds one.

What it is, without the jargon

You almost certainly already use an assistant that can answer general questions about money. It can explain what a Lifetime ISA is. It cannot tell you whether you paid into one this year, because it has never seen your accounts.

earmarkIQ Context closes that gap in the narrowest way available. earmarkIQ already connects to your UK bank accounts through read-only Open Banking and organises what it finds — categorising your spending, tracking your subscriptions, working out your net worth. Context makes that organised picture readable by an assistant you already use, and nothing more than readable.

The technical name for the plug is MCP, the Model Context Protocol: an open standard for connecting assistants to outside sources of information with your permission. If you want the fuller explanation, we have written what MCP is and why it matters for your money. For the purposes of this page, the only thing that matters is what the connection permits — and that is a short list.

What your assistant can read

This is the complete surface. An assistant connected through Context can ask for these things and nothing else, because nothing else is published for it to find.

  • ✓Accounts and balances — every account you have connected to earmarkIQ, in one picture
  • ✓Spending by category — your transactions as earmarkIQ has categorised them
  • ✓Subscriptions and recurring payments — including price changes earmarkIQ has detected
  • ✓Net worth and property equity — including your indexed property valuation and mortgage balance
  • ✓Your payday allocation plan — the split earmarkIQ builds for you each payday

Which makes questions like these answerable from your actual position rather than a general one:

"What did I spend on eating out last month?"
"Which of my subscriptions went up this year, and by how much?"
"How much equity do I have in the flat, and how has my net worth moved since January?"
"Given what I usually spend, is a £900 holiday in March realistic?"

What it cannot do

The absences are the point, so here they are explicitly:

  • ✕It cannot move money — between your accounts or anywhere else
  • ✕It cannot make or authorise a payment
  • ✕It cannot cancel a subscription or a direct debit — it can find one and tell you what it costs
  • ✕It cannot change any setting — in earmarkIQ or at your bank
  • ✕It cannot reach your bank — it reads earmarkIQ's picture, never your bank connection
  • ✕It cannot see your banking credentials — nobody can, including us; Open Banking authenticates you at your own bank

How the read-only guarantee is enforced

"Read-only" is claimed constantly and means different things. Sometimes it means a capability exists and is switched off. Sometimes it means a model has been instructed not to use it. Neither is worth much: switches get flipped and instructions get talked around.

What we mean is that the capability does not exist, and there are three independent layers holding that in place.

01

The permission layer

earmarkIQ's bank connection runs under Account Information Services — a reading permission — through Finexer Ltd (FRN 925695), an FCA-authorised provider. AIS has no ability to construct a payment instruction. So even before Context enters the picture, there is no payment capability upstream for anything to reach. How AIS and PIS differ →

02

The surface layer

An MCP server publishes a list of operations, and an assistant can invoke what is on that list and nothing else. Context's list contains read operations only. There is no mutating operation for an assistant to call, badly-worded prompt to trigger, or instruction to be argued into — because there is nothing there.

03

The build layer

The reason a guarantee erodes is rarely a decision. It is a change that seemed reasonable at the time. So the constraint is enforced in the build itself: an automated test fails if any mutating capability is ever added to Context. Adding one is not a thing that can be done quietly — it breaks the build, visibly, and someone has to consciously remove the test to proceed.

On top of those three, two things give you visibility rather than assurances:

  • Every access is audited. Requests made through Context are recorded, so "we would never" can be replaced with a record of what actually happened.
  • Revocation is yours. You can end the connection yourself, immediately, without contacting anyone. See how to revoke.
🔒 The guarantee, stated exactly

An assistant connected through earmarkIQ Context can read the five categories listed above. It cannot move money, make a payment, cancel anything, or change any setting, because no such capability exists in Context to be invoked. This is enforced by the permission earmarkIQ operates under, by the operations Context publishes, and by a test that fails the build if a mutating capability is added.

The limit worth knowing about

Context controls what an assistant can read from earmarkIQ. It has no control over the assistant itself. Once an answer about your finances appears in a conversation, that conversation sits with whoever provides the assistant, under their retention and privacy terms.

Read-only stops your money being moved. It does not make the conversation private from the assistant's provider, and no server design can. If your financial position appearing in a chat log is a problem for you, that is a genuine reason not to connect — and it is a question about the assistant, not about Context.

How to connect

Get your Context address

Your personal earmarkIQ Context address comes from your earmarkIQ account. It is yours alone and reads only your data.

Add it as a custom connector

In your MCP-capable assistant, add earmarkIQ Context as a custom connector using that address. Claude is verified. Other MCP-capable assistants connect the same way, though we have not verified each one individually.

Approve read-only access

Your assistant will ask to connect. Approve it, and it can start answering from your data. Your bank connection and credentials stay with earmarkIQ, and the assistant never sees either.

How to revoke

Two independent routes, both immediate, neither requiring you to contact us:

  • From earmarkIQ. Revoke Context access in your earmarkIQ account. The connection ends at that moment.
  • From your assistant. Remove the connector. It can no longer reach anything.

Revoking Context changes nothing else. Your earmarkIQ account, your categorised data and your bank connections all carry on exactly as before — you have removed one reader, not unpicked anything. You can reconnect later if you want to.

Questions

What is an MCP server? ›
MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard that lets AI assistants such as Claude connect securely to outside sources of information, with your permission. An MCP server is the bridge: it publishes a specific list of things an assistant may ask for, and nothing outside that list exists to be discovered. earmarkIQ Context is that bridge for your finances, so answers are grounded in your real numbers rather than general knowledge.
Can an assistant connected via Context move my money? ›
No. Context is read-only by construction: it publishes read operations only, and no capability to move money, make a payment, cancel a subscription or change a setting exists within it. This is enforced structurally rather than by policy — a test in the earmarkIQ build fails if any mutating capability is ever added. earmarkIQ itself also operates under Account Information Services, a read permission, so there is no payment capability upstream either.
How is the read-only guarantee actually enforced? ›
In three layers. First, the permission layer: earmarkIQ's bank connection runs under Account Information Services via Finexer Ltd (FRN 925695), which is a reading permission with no ability to construct a payment instruction. Second, the surface layer: Context publishes only read operations, so there is no mutating capability for an assistant to invoke. Third, the build layer: an automated test fails the build if any mutating capability is added to Context, so the guarantee cannot be eroded quietly over time. Every access is also recorded in an audit trail visible to you.
Which assistants work with it? ›
Claude is verified. Because MCP is an open standard and Context is a standard MCP server, other MCP-capable assistants that support custom connectors can connect the same way, but we have not verified each one individually and do not claim compatibility we have not tested.
Does this make my conversation private? ›
No, and it is important to be clear about it. Context controls what an assistant can read from earmarkIQ. It has no control over the assistant itself: once an answer about your finances appears in a conversation, that conversation sits with whoever provides the assistant, under their retention and privacy terms. Read-only prevents your money being moved. It does not make the conversation private from the assistant's provider, and no server design can.
How do I disconnect? ›
Revoke Context access from your earmarkIQ account at any time, or remove the connector from your assistant. Access ends there and then — nothing about your earmarkIQ app or your bank connections is affected. You do not need to contact support and there is no notice period.
Does earmarkIQ initiate payments? ›
No. earmarkIQ does not initiate payments today. Payment initiation sits within Finexer's permissions rather than earmarkIQ's and is on the roadmap, but the capability is not enabled. If it is ever switched on, it would be a payment you start, with an amount and payee you see and confirm, authenticated by you at your own bank, one payment at a time. earmarkIQ Context would remain read-only, and no assistant would be able to trigger a payment.
